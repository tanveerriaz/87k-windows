When we asked my parents about their younger lives, something changed. My father spoke of the love and example that shaped him. My mother remembered the small things that made ordinary days joyful. They were not simply looking back. They were sharing something that could still matter.

Every family and neighbourhood holds experiences like these. But the people who could learn from them, or find comfort in hearing them, often never know they exist.

87K Windows turns lived experience into a reason for two people to meet. It begins with one gentle question: What small thing made you happy when you were young?

Local Gemma listens for what the memory can offer: a place, a time, a skill, an interest, something the person may want to share. The participant reviews that interpretation and decides what may leave their hands.

One person remembers repairing radios in Queenstown. Another wants to understand how old radios worked. Transparent matching sees the connection: one remembers how; another wants to learn.

After a real match, Gemini prepares two optional questions and a reminder that either person may pause or stop. AI does not become the relationship. It creates a safe beginning, then steps away.

If the evidence is weak, there is no invitation. A connection should begin with something real, or wait until the right listener arrives.

A lifetime of experience should not remain hidden behind one window. When someone is ready to share and someone else is ready to receive, memory becomes useful, two people meet, and a neighbourhood becomes less lonely.
